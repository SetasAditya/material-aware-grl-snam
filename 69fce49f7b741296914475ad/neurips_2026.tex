\begin{abstract}
Geometry-only navigation treats two equally clear routes as equivalent even when they differ substantially in traversal risk, such as firm ground versus wet clay. Adding a material-risk potential addresses this ambiguity, but an always-exposed risk gradient can steer toward a lower-risk direction that is not locally traversable. Material-aware navigation therefore requires controlling both when material information influences motion and how strongly it does so. We augment a port-Hamiltonian navigator with a separate material-risk force channel whose roles are deliberately decomposed into a direction given by the material-risk gradient, a context-conditioned magnitude, and a local feasibility witness that gates exposure. The witness provides evidence that a feasible lower-risk motion exists rather than a reference trajectory, and the Hamiltonian field generates the executed motion. The learned contextual coefficients are trained with a CVaR objective that emphasizes high-cost tail rollouts, while feasibility remains determined by the local witness. This separation enables direct intervention on each component. On delayed-required-escape episodes, the full method reduces premature activation from 0.950 to 0.380 and raises success from 0.480 to 0.610 with zero replans, and toggling only the gate on a fixed trained model removes 91--94\% of the soft-force exposure the ungated field would admit. On DFC2018, isolating the material channel reduces cumulative material risk from 10.340 to 10.288 without reducing success. The learned magnitude also adapts across domains without domain-specific retuning, from approximately 0.11 on RELLIS to approximately 1.81 on DFC2018. We report two limits alongside these gains. Retraining the same architecture and gate under expected cost rather than CVaR separates no outcome metric at 95\% confidence, so the selectivity is attributable to the gate rather than to the objective. The hard-hazard barrier is differentiable but is not a control-barrier certificate, so safety claims refer to empirical finite-rollout violation reductions rather than formal guarantees.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Giving material risk its own additive force channel, gated by a local feasibility
witness, gives a navigation policy a selectivity signature that can be measured
rather than inferred from trajectories. The material response is admitted when
a feasible lower-risk motion exists nearby and withheld otherwise. Because
direction, strength, and exposure are separate factors, each can be intervened on
independently, and the interventions localize the behavior to the gate. Holding
the trained model fixed and toggling only the gate removes $91$--$94\%$ of the
soft-force exposure the ungated field would admit, without altering the executed
path, and against a reactive
baseline on the delayed-required-escape benchmark the gated field cuts premature
activation from $0.950$ to $0.380$ while raising success from $0.480$ to $0.610$
with zero replans. The context-conditioned magnitude spans roughly a
$17{\times}$ range between off-road and dense-semantic domains with no
per-domain retuning. The witness is loose on path, in that the executed step
differs from the witness ray by a median $45^\circ$, yet it transfers on the
properties that matter, agreeing on clearance and hard contact after the escape
opens.

Two results bound these claims. The CVaR objective is not what produces the
selectivity. Retraining the same architecture and gate under expected cost
separates no outcome metric at $95\%$ confidence over $100$ paired episodes, so
we report CVaR as the training rule we used rather than as a demonstrated
contributor. And robustness to perception degradation is conservatism, not
discrimination. As semantic labels are corrupted, activations that were correct
on the clean map and those that were false decay almost identically
($0.414$ vs.\ $0.446$ retained at $30\%$ corruption). The construction also
offers empirical reductions rather than certificates, the gate is frame-wise and
can flicker, and reactive boundary-following remains stronger on
moving-obstacle events. The mechanism is a way to make one channel's influence
auditable; the natural next steps are temporal hysteresis in the witness,
calibrated perception uncertainty in the gate conditions, and testing the same
gated channel inside other vector-field navigators.


\paragraph{Social Impact Statement.}
This work uses public or simulated navigation data and does not rely on private user information. Its intended benefit is more reliable autonomous navigation under terrain and traffic uncertainty. The method is not a deployment safety certificate, and real systems would require independent validation, fallback control, and monitoring.
